# 1 Thoughts
1. This is an easy book to read — very *fast-paced*, which is actually unusual for Sanderson. I’ve always thought of his books like a *sine wave*: long stretches where you question your life choices for picking the book, followed by a massive BANG — a plot twist you absolutely didn’t see coming. This book, though, is more like *y = c* — constant pace throughout. And… I can’t decide if that’s good or bad. It’s definitely **more readable, but it also flattens the payoff.**  
2. The action is fun, no doubt. But it just didn’t have that Sanderson magic for me. The character development is pretty weak. There are some decent twists toward the end — even though I saw most of them coming (probably because it feels **very similar to Mistborn #1: Kelsier → Prof, Vin → David, Lord Ruler → Steelheart, the crew dynamic, etc.)**. The Megan twist was the only one that genuinely worked for me. But beyond that, *we barely learn anything meaningful about the rest of the crew.* Maybe that’s saved for later books, but right now, there’s just not enough emotional investment.  
3. The whole thing feels very… **boyish** (part of the setup is also similar to The Boys, lol). Like a Hardy Boys or Famous Five adventure — fast, convenient, and always moving. Every problem conveniently introduces its own solution within a few pages. There’s very little tension that lingers.  
4. That said, the prologue and opening are fantastic. No wasted time. Straight into it. And that first line — ***“I saw Steelheart bleed.”*** — ooof, that’s classic Sanderson hook. There are also moments where the crew pauses to question why they’re doing what they’re doing, and those bits are genuinely interesting. I just wish there were more of them.  
5. But honestly, a lot of the rest veers into **cringe** territory. The randomness of the powers annoyed me. Even more so, the weaknesses of the Epics. One of the things I admire most about Sanderson is his tight, almost scientific *magic systems. Here, it feels… arbitrary*. Like, you can only be hurt if exactly five people attack you? Or based on how someone feels about you? Come on, what is this?  
6. There are also some tired tropes that don’t land well — especially the **stereotypical** “guy noticing girl” writing. The **[[world-building]] is surprisingly thin too**. Apart from the steel-covered city (which is cool), it’s basically our world. And yet the characters behave like they’ve lived in a completely alien reality for generations. It’s only been ~10 years, but there’s barely any reference to the “old world,” which feels odd.  
7. David as a **protagonist** is also pretty **cliché**. Revenge-driven, slightly awkward nerd… who is also conveniently athletic, resilient, and always ends up outperforming others in high-pressure situations. It feels a bit too ***convenient***. The Epics themselves are another missed opportunity — apart from a couple, they don’t feel like a structured power group. More like chaotic individuals drunk on power.  
8. Cody is clearly there for comic relief, but the humor didn’t land for me. Leaning on Scottish/Australian stereotypes isn’t exactly peak comedy.  
9. And structurally, the plot feels very Final Empire-ish — assembling a crew, taking down an overpowered ruler, etc. But it never reaches the same depth or payoff. This is definitely not the Sanderson book I’d recommend as someone’s first.  
10. *Megan, though — that was a nice touch. The reveal works*, and looking back, some of her earlier lines hit differently in a good way. That was probably the one moment where the book felt layered.  
  
If I had to sum it up:  
Fun, fast, and readable — but missing the depth, structure, and magic that make Sanderson great. and it's funny that all Sanderson fans would probably rate this lower than first time sanderson readers...purely because he has raised expectations to a whole new level.
