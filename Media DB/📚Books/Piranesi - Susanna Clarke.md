### 0.1.1 Intro for the bookclub
Thanks to everyone for reading and coming. Reading is becoming so rare and I think it's important to acknowledge why we read and what's the fun of all these meetings. We've all heard the escapism and probably a natural tendency in humans to like stories, but it's also about looking at things from a different perspective and living someone else's life when your years are probably limited. Bhavya mentioned the other day, there's so much on our tbr lists and so little time. I would say that's more true for life. I don't wanna be just an engineer. I want to try and be a doctor, a painter, a rockstar, a statistician for a football club, i wanna have my own show on NatGeo where i draw maps, find out wildlife and spend my life searching some rare monster that hides in the forests somewhere. And I can't do all of that, so when I read or i watch content i feel i've lived some of that on my own. 

# 1 Points to discuss
1. The beauty of being a minority
2. **Talking to birds** - you can think Piranesi is insane. But he explains it and it seems logical. We all talk to birds. We shoo them away, we sometimes imitate their voices (i for sure have imitated the cuckoo bird's voice in my old house and i'm damn sure it used to reply back), we just do stuff without even noticing. So, ***Piranesi is probably not so crazy after all.*** We talk to dogs, and babies.
3. Why does **Piranesi think there's something the birds are trying to tell him**. Have you ever done this in your life? If you have, we're already creating small fantasy words of our own and that's probably why we talk our pets and we love toy story. The author, `Susanne Clarke` didnt originally put that sequence in. She went to a writing school where she met her husband as well and one of the tutors talked about the fact that we dont know where birds come from and very easily they could come from another world and when they fly maybe they are entering a different world - maybe not literally but in some way. So, the author had Piranesi talk to the birds at the end, but someone pointed this out to them that why he does that and she added in more sequences of Piranesi interacting with the birds and interpreting them because he thinks they are from a different world carrying messages from there.
4. Imagining a world - I was seeing this podcast by `chalchitratalks` the other day with `Konkona sen`, and `Vaibhav`, the host said an interesting thing. He said when he's reading he sometimes would consciously ***read on his own terms.*** **if the author says it's day, he'll imagine night and see where that takes him.** It's silly but it's also something beautiful. And that is something you can do with books, and probably not movies and even less with short form content. We usually switch off our imagination when consuming visual content, so it's sometimes important to read once in a while and that is one of the reasons i love fantasy. I just fabled by the way some people in this world can think. How they can spin a new world with new rules and explain it to a reader, someone who has no idea of how the author thinks. I dont think another species can do that. I doubt a dog thinks what would it be like if it were stuck in a labrynth and start talking to birds.
5. **Being afraid of accepting reality or being positive?** I think one reason to love Piranesi is that he tries to approach very harsh realities in a positive manner. *2 To Ramble* compare Piranesi to a kid with cancer, who are different from adults. They know it's gonna happen, but they create this reasoning for themselves and sort of a fantasy world that gives them strength. One way is to look at it and say it's naiive, but it's also beautiful.
6. **Piranesi is not the one who's afraid. Kimberley is.** He has a way to ***escape*** and Piranesi is the one who's stuck and yet Piranesi finds freedom and beauty in the House, in his own world. What if u are ***stuck in traffic***. Look how blessed i am that the world has slowed down to give me time to look at the world and maybe play a game with the number plates. Something you would never do on your own, finding time to appreciate the little things.
7. **No jealousy** towards Kimberley who has the best clothes, well fed. Piranesi thinks the House gives him everything because he can't fend for himself. 
8. **Imagining the character**. For the longest time i thought of ***Piranesi as not human***. It was only when Kimberley brings in the gun, i thought this is actually a world very similar to ours. Not the statues, not anything else.
9. **Ignorance is bliss or knowledge is power?** Piranesi lives somewhere in between. He looks at both of them, always seeking new knowledge. READ OUT HOW PIRANESI FOUND OUT ABOUT KIMBERLEY. Shows how he thinks, such a brilliant exercise in putting words to one's thoughts. He still describes everything so articulately. He still tries to pull him up and save him when the flood comes in. Does patriotism try to push us in the opposite direction.
10. **The year of weeping and wailing**. How Piranesi names the ***chapters***. Why is that one chapter named differently?
11. How should we treat our world? : Piranesi's exercise of **appreciating the house** is like the act of praying, you just say thank you.
12. Where does the name Piranesi come from? Giovanni Piranesi, an artist. the book is based on another author's work, who talks about labyrinth - check podcast.
13. Being the only one who understands and knows a place - Inspiration from Earthsea, the tombs in that book and how eventhough Piranesi's world is not as dark and full of statues and some life, it can be lonely. But the beauty of having a special place that only you understand or only u understand in the way you do, and how beautiful that is. The place becomes a part of u. That tension of thinking the world is a part of him, he calls himself the son of the house.
14. Keeping a bit of Piranesi with me. I go feed the birds. If i wake up early, i try and sit and stare at the dawn and i feel Piranesi would want me to do this. I miss him now that he's not here.
15. **How does Piranesi think he knows so much about the world**. That the ***watch will get wet*** and destroyed, that he knows how to write, that he knows what an ***albatross*** is. Do we think about how we know the world like we know It today? would it be different if certain things had played out differently? it relates to *Chomskii* and how he talks about humans learning the world and our language in some sense.
